Topic III · Manuscript pages 15–19

Half-open sides and image-edge half-plane intersections

When a contact lands at a vertex, how do we assign it to exactly one of the two incident sides?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

A contact in the relative interior of a side belongs to only that side, but a polygon vertex belongs to two closed sides. Replacing each closed side [xᵢ₋₁,xᵢ] by the half-open side (xᵢ₋₁,xᵢ] assigns every boundary point to exactly one side. Definition 4.2 and Lemmas 4.3–4.7 develop this convention and the local vertex replacement used in Topic IV.

The second strand begins with clipping along an edge of the image polygon. Clipping preserves invariance and bounds the number of vertices of the clipped polygon. Compactness of the normalized class and continuity of area yield a least-area member; strict area monotonicity is then used to rule out proper normalized cuts and prove the boundary-arc bound.

Reading convention. Essential definitions appear before the first statement that needs them. Complete manuscript proofs are closed by default. When a result has an added line-by-line explanation, it appears inside the same disclosure as the proof.

III-A · Half-open side assignments

Assign every boundary point to exactly one side

The half-open sides Ei+=(xi−1,xi] form a disjoint partition of ∂P. Supporting functionals and determinant inequalities then identify the relevant side without introducing another geometric relation. Definition 4.2 and Lemmas 4.3–4.7 form the first strand of this chapter: they prepare the endpoint-order argument in Topic IV and are not used to prove the clipping bound below.

  1. 4.2
    Definition 4.2

    Right-half-open side convention

    Define which one of the two incident sides receives a boundary point that is exactly a polygon vertex.

    Definitions and notation used here5 entries to consult as needed
    Side index i
    The index i labels the side Ei=[xi−1,xi]. All vertex and side indices are read modulo N, so an index that passes N−1 continues again at 0.
    Incoming side
    At vertex xi, side Ei arrives when the boundary is traversed positively; Ei+1 leaves.
    Half-open side
    (xi−1,xi] excludes its left endpoint and includes its right endpoint.
    Open an example
    Two closed sides share xi. Their half-open versions do not: the incoming side includes it and the outgoing side excludes it.
    Assigned to a side
    A point is assigned to side index i precisely when it belongs to Ei+. This is only the membership statement z∈Ei+; it introduces no additional geometric relation.
    Image vertex and half-open side assignment
    If Q=λP, an image vertex is a point λxj obtained from a vertex of P. Assigning that image vertex to side index i means proving λxj∈Ei+. Definition 4.2 fixes what happens at an endpoint; it does not yet prove a simultaneous assignment for all image vertices.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionA point in the relative interior of a side belongs to only that side. At a vertex, two closed sides overlap. Excluding each side’s left endpoint and including its right endpoint removes that overlap everywhere around the boundary.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Definition 4.2 (Right-half-open side convention). For a positively oriented polygon with its complete cyclic vertex list, side index ii refers to the half-open side Ei+=(xi−1,xi].E_i^+=(x_{i-1},x_i]. Thus a polygon vertex xix_i belongs to the incoming half-open side Ei+E_i^+, not to the outgoing half-open side Ei+1+E_{i+1}^+. More generally, assigning a boundary point z∈∂Pz\in\partial P to side index ii means precisely that z∈Ei+.z\in E_i^+. In particular, assigning an image vertex ξi\xi_i to side index ii means ξi∈Ei+\xi_i\in E_i^+. Either ξi∈relint⁡(Ei)\xi_i\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}(E_i), in which case xi−1,ξi,xix_{i-1},\xi_i,x_i occur in that positive boundary order, or ξi=xi\xi_i=x_i. If two consecutive image vertices yj,yj+1y_j,y_{j+1} both lie on ∂P\partial P, the intervening boundary arc follows the same endpoint convention: the positive half-open arc (yj,yj+1]∂P(y_j,y_{j+1}]_{\partial P} includes its right endpoint and excludes its left endpoint.

    Key conclusionThis defines a boundary partition only; a simultaneous assignment of all image vertices still has to be proved.

  2. 4.3
    Lemma 4.3Classical result

    Half-open sides partition the boundary

    Prove that every boundary point belongs to exactly one half-open side and identify which side determinants vanish there.

    Definitions and notation used here2 entries to consult as needed
    Oriented side determinant
    The value Di(z) tests on which side of the directed line xi−1→xi the point z lies. Positive orientation makes membership in the polygon equivalent to all these values being nonnegative.
    Indices of side lines through z
    Z(z)={r : Dr(z)=0} is the set of side indices whose lines contain z. A point in the relative interior of one side gives one index; a vertex gives the two indices of its incident sides.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionThe equations Dr(z)=0 say exactly which side lines pass through z. One vanishing determinant identifies a side-interior point; two adjacent ones identify a vertex. At that vertex, the half-open convention retains only the incoming side.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.3 (Half-open sides partition the boundary). Let PP be a positively oriented NN-gon with complete vertex list xix_i and oriented sides Ei=[xi−1,xi]E_i=[x_{i-1},x_i]. For Di(z)=det⁡(xi−xi−1,z−xi−1),D_i(z)=\det(x_i-x_{i-1},z-x_{i-1}), the following statements hold.

    1. The half-open sides Ei+=(xi−1,xi]E_i^+=(x_{i-1},x_i] are pairwise disjoint and form a partition of ∂P\partial P.

    2. If z∈Ei+z\in E_i^+, then the vanishing-index set Z(z)={r:Dr(z)=0}Z(z)=\{r:D_r(z)=0\} is Z(z)={i}for z∈relint⁡(Ei),Z(xi)={i,i+1}.Z(z)=\{i\}\quad\text{for }z\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}(E_i), \qquad Z(x_i)=\{i,i+1\}.

    3. If z∈Pz\in P and Di(z)=0D_i(z)=0, then z∈Eiz\in E_i.

    4. If z∈Ei+z\in E_i^+ and z∈Ej+z\in E_j^+, then i=ji=j.

    Complete proofOpen the complete manuscript proof

    Proof. The positively oriented side inequalities give the half-plane and face representations P=⋂i{z:Di(z)≥0},P∩{z:Di(z)=0}=Ei.P=\bigcap_i\{z:D_i(z)\geq0\}, \qquad P\cap\{z:D_i(z)=0\}=E_i.   3. 4.4
    Lemma 4.4Classical result

    A boundary convex combination lies on one side

    Show that an interior point of a segment can reach a convex boundary only when the whole segment lies in one boundary face.

    Definitions and notation used here2 entries to consult as needed
    Strict convex combination
    (1−s)A+sB with 0<s<1; it is a point strictly between A and B.
    Supporting affine functional
    A nonconstant affine function f(z)=ℓ(z)+b, with ℓ≠0, that is bounded above by c on the polygon and equals c on the touched boundary face.
    Open an example
    Imagine parallel level lines moving toward a polygon. The first level line to touch it exposes a vertex or a side.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionA supporting functional cannot average two values below its maximum and somehow obtain the maximum. Both endpoint values must already be maximal.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.4 (A strict convex combination on the boundary lies in one side). Let RR be a convex polygon with nonempty interior, let A,B∈RA,B\in R with A≠BA\neq B, and let 0<s<10<s<1. If (1−s)A+sB∈∂R,(1-s)A+sB\in\partial R, then AA and BB lie on one common side of RR, and the whole segment [A,B][A,B] is contained in that side.

    Complete proofOpen the complete manuscript proof

    Proof. Choose a nonconstant supporting affine functional f≤cf\leq c for RR at z=(1−s)A+sBz=(1-s)A+sB. Since c=f(z)=(1−s)f(A)+sf(B),f(A),f(B)≤c,c=f(z)=(1-s)f(A)+sf(B), \qquad f(A),f(B)\leq c, positivity of both coefficients forces f(A)=f(B)=cf(A)=f(B)=c. Thus AA and BB belong to the exposed face R∩{f=c}R\cap\{f=c\}. This face is nondegenerate because A≠BA\neq B, so it is a side of the polygon and contains [A,B][A,B]. ◻

    Lemma 4.5Classical result

    Locating the common side from adjacent half-open memberships

    Use two adjacent half-open side memberships to determine the common side and the forced endpoint.

    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionThe previous lemma gives one common side. The possible vanishing indices for A and B have only j in common, so that side must be Ej. This forces A to be the shared vertex xj−1.

    Lemma 4.5 (Locating the common side from adjacent half-open memberships). Let PP be a positively oriented polygon.   5. 4.6
    Lemma 4.6Classical result

    Determinant test for side membership and containment

    Replace the containment claim by a finite matrix of oriented determinant inequalities.

    Definitions and notation used here2 entries to consult as needed
    Determinant matrix
    The entry Dr,j tests the selected point ηj against the supporting half-plane of side Er. Thus row r checks one side inequality and column j checks one point.
    Bilinearity of the determinant
    The determinant is linear in each vector separately; therefore its value at a convex combination of points is the same convex combination of their determinant values.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionIf every selected point satisfies every oriented side inequality, all of them lie in the polygon. The prescribed zero in column j simultaneously records its contact with side j.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.6 (Determinant test for side membership and containment). Let PP be a positively oriented NN-gon with its complete displayed vertex list xix_i and half-open sides Ei+E_i^+. Let ηj\eta_j be NN points such that, for every jj, either ηj=xj,\eta_j=x_j, or ηj=(1−tj)xj−1+tjxj,0<tj<1.\eta_j=(1-t_j)x_{j-1}+t_jx_j, \qquad 0<t_j<1. Put Cr,k=det⁡(xr−xr−1,xk−xr−1),C_{r,k}=\det(x_r-x_{r-1},x_k-x_{r-1}), so Cr,r−1=Cr,r=0C_{r,r-1}=C_{r,r}=0, and put Dr,j=det⁡(xr−xr−1,ηj−xr−1).D_{r,j}=\det(x_r-x_{r-1},\eta_j-x_{r-1}). Then ηj∈Ej+\eta_j\in E_j^+ for every jj and Dr,j≥0(0≤r,j<N).D_{r,j}\ge0\qquad(0\le r,j<N).     Complete proofOpen the complete manuscript proof

    Proof. The endpoint xjx_j belongs to Ej+E_j^+ and to no other half-open side, while a strict convex combination of xj−1x_{j-1} and xjx_j belongs to relint⁡(Ej)⊂Ej+\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}(E_j)\subset E_j^+.   6. 4.7
    Lemma 4.7

    Side membership after replacing one vertex

    Check the local half-open side memberships after one vertex is replaced by a point in the relative interior of an adjacent side.

    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionAfter replacing xi by ξi, the new point is the right endpoint of its incoming side and the excluded left endpoint of the next side. The same convention protects the unchanged endpoint at xi+1.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.7 (Side membership after replacing one vertex). Let PP be a positively oriented NN-gon with complete vertex list xrx_r and sides Er=[xr−1,xr]E_r=[x_{r-1},x_r]. Choose ξi∈relint⁡(Ei)\xi_i\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}(E_i) and define P′=conv⁡((Ext⁡(P)\{xi})∪{ξi}).P'=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Bigl((\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P)\setminus\{x_i\})\cup\{\xi_i\}\Bigr). Then P′P' is an NN-gon whose complete positive cyclic vertex list is obtained from that of PP by replacing xix_i by xi′=ξix'_i=\xi_i. In particular, the points xi−1,xi′=ξi,xi+1x_{i-1},x'_i=\xi_i,x_{i+1} occur in that positive cyclic order. For the two new sides Ei′=[xi−1,ξi],Ei+1′=[ξi,xi+1],E_i'=[x_{i-1},\xi_i], \qquad E_{i+1}'=[\xi_i,x_{i+1}], one has ξi∈Ei′+,xi+1∈Ei+1′+.\xi_i\in E_i^{\prime +}, \qquad x_{i+1}\in E_{i+1}^{\prime +}.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. There is a unique t∈(0,1)t\in(0,1) such that ξi=(1−t)xi−1+txi.\xi_i=(1-t)x_{i-1}+tx_i. Let L=aff⁡(xi−1,xi+1)L=\mathop{\mathrm{aff}}(x_{i-1},x_{i+1}). Convexity and the completeness of the vertex list imply that xix_i lies in one open half-plane bounded by LL, whereas every other vertex except xi−1,xi+1x_{i-1},x_{i+1} lies in the opposite open half-plane. Since ξi=(1−t)xi−1+txi\xi_i=(1-t)x_{i-1}+tx_i with t>0t>0, the point ξi\xi_i lies in the same open half-plane as xix_i. It therefore does not belong to the convex hull of the unchanged vertices, so it is an extreme point of P′P'.

    Each unchanged vertex remains extreme: a linear functional that strictly exposes it on PP still strictly exposes it among the displayed points defining P′P' (its value at ξi∈P\xi_i\in P is also strictly smaller). Thus all NN displayed points are extreme points of P′P'.

    The supporting line of the old side EiE_i meets these displayed points only at xi−1x_{i-1} and ξi\xi_i, so [xi−1,ξi][x_{i-1},\xi_i] is an exposed side of P′P'. For every unchanged vertex xk≠xi+1x_k\neq x_{i+1}, the function ξ↦det⁡(xi+1−ξ,xk−ξ)\xi\mapsto\det(x_{i+1}-\xi,x_k-\xi) is affine in ξ\xi. Hence det⁡(xi+1−ξi,xk−ξi)=(1−t)det⁡(xi+1−xi−1,xk−xi−1)+tdet⁡(xi+1−xi,xk−xi)>0.\det(x_{i+1}-\xi_i,x_k-\xi_i) =(1-t)\det(x_{i+1}-x_{i-1},x_k-x_{i-1}) +t\det(x_{i+1}-x_i,x_k-x_i)>0. The triple-sign criterion makes both terms nonnegative and at least one strictly positive (when xk=xi−1x_k=x_{i-1}, the first term is zero and the second is positive). Hence all the other displayed points lie strictly in the left half-plane of the directed line from ξi\xi_i to xi+1x_{i+1}, so [ξi,xi+1][\xi_i,x_{i+1}] is also an exposed side of P′P'. Every old side not incident with xix_i remains exposed by its old supporting line. These sides join all NN extreme points in the displayed positive cyclic order. Consequently that list is exactly the complete positive cyclic vertex list of P′P'; in particular, P′P' is an NN-gon and the two sides incident with ξi\xi_i are Ei′=[xi−1,ξi]E_i'=[x_{i-1},\xi_i] and Ei+1′=[ξi,xi+1]E_{i+1}'=[\xi_i,x_{i+1}].

    The point ξi\xi_i is the right endpoint of Ei′E_i' and the left endpoint of Ei+1′E_{i+1}', so the right-half-open convention assigns it to Ei′+E_i^{\prime +}. The point xi+1x_{i+1} is the right endpoint of Ei+1′E_{i+1}', so it belongs to Ei+1′+E_{i+1}^{\prime +}. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Define the modified polygon
      Replace xi by the chosen point ξi in the relative interior of the incoming side, and take the convex hull of the resulting N points.
    2. 2
      Verify the new boundary
      Supporting lines and determinant signs show that all N displayed points remain extreme, in the same cyclic order. Hence the adjacent new sides are [xi−1,ξi] and [ξi,xi+1].
    3. 3
      Apply the right-half-open convention
      Each displayed shared endpoint belongs only to the side arriving there.

    Key conclusionReplacing one vertex changes only the two adjacent sides; the half-open endpoint convention determines both local memberships without a global index shift.

III-B · Edge clipping

Clip along an actual edge of the image polygon

This begins the second strand. The retained half-plane contains the entire image polygon. That single containment preserves invariance and leaves an exact count of the vertices that remain and the possible new endpoints.

  1. 4.8
    Lemma 4.8Previously known

    Clipping along an image edge and bounding the number of vertices

    Cut along a genuine edge of the image polygon while preserving invariance and counting every lost or introduced vertex.

    Definitions and notation used here5 entries to consult as needed
    Image polygon
    Q=λP is obtained by applying the multiplier to every point of P. Because λ≠0, its vertices correspond bijectively to those of P.
    Clipped polygon and removed region
    The line through one edge of Q bounds a closed half-plane Hj containing Q. The clipped polygon is Pj=P∩Hj; the two-dimensional part removed from P is P∖Pj.
    Positive closed and open boundary arcs
    For boundary points a and b, the positive closed arc from a to b is the part of ∂P traversed from a to b in the positive orientation, with both endpoints included. Its open arc is obtained by deleting those endpoints.
    Discarded arc and old-vertex count
    The discarded closed arc Aj is the boundary path cut off by the image edge, not the two-dimensional removed region. Its open part is Aj∘, and kj=#(Ext(P)∩Aj) counts the old vertices on the closed arc.
    Image edge on a supporting line of P
    If the line containing an edge of Q also supports P, then P already lies in the retained half-plane and Pj=P; no region is removed.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionThe retained half-plane contains all of Q. Hence it also contains the image of the smaller clipped polygon. To bound the number of vertices, remove the old vertices on the discarded boundary arc and add an edge endpoint only when it was not already a vertex of P.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.8 (Clipping along an image edge and bounding the number of vertices). Let PP be an NN-gon, let λ≠0\lambda\neq0, and put Q=λPQ=\lambda P. Assume Q⊆PQ\subseteq P and that every vertex of QQ lies on ∂P\partial P. List the vertices of QQ in their positive cyclic order as y0,…,yN−1y_0,\ldots,y_{N-1}. Their order on ∂P\partial P is the same cyclic order.

    Proof.       Preserve cyclic order

III-C · The least-area invariant polygon

Existence of a least-area normalized polygon and the boundary-arc bound

The appendix tools are placed where they are needed. Their numbering remains unchanged, and the boundary-arc bound follows only after the existence of a least-area normalized invariant N-gon has been proved.

  1. A.4
    Lemma A.4Classical result

    Hausdorff limits of finitely generated polygons

    Prove that labelled vertex convergence controls the whole polygon and preserves every closed condition needed by the area minimization.

    Definitions and notation used here6 entries to consult as needed
    Hausdorff distance
    The least ε such that every point of either compact set lies within distance ε of the other. Both directions are essential.
    Open an example
    A set cannot converge in Hausdorff distance merely by lying near the limit; the limit must also lie near the approximating set.
    Sequential compactness
    A set is sequentially compact when every sequence in it has a convergent subsequence whose limit still lies in the set. In finite-dimensional Euclidean space, every closed bounded set—and every finite product of such sets—has this property.
    Support function
    hK(u) is the largest value of the linear functional z↦⟨u,z⟩ on K.
    Indicator function
    The function equal to 1 on a set and 0 outside it; integrating it gives planar area.
    Dominated convergence theorem
    If measurable functions converge at almost every point and one integrable function bounds all their absolute values, then their integrals converge to the integral of the limit. Here one fixed disk supplies the bound.
    Area-zero boundary
    A finite union of line segments has planar area zero. Consequently, pointwise convergence may fail on a polygon boundary without affecting the area integral.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionMatching the same convex coefficients in the approximating and limiting vertex lists gives an immediate two-sided distance bound. The deeper point is that interior points eventually stay inside, enabling area convergence.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma A.4 (Hausdorff limits of finitely generated polygons). Fix NN and let Pk=conv⁡{zk,1,…,zk,N}P_k=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{z_{k,1},\ldots,z_{k,N}\} with all points in one compact subset of ℝ2\mathbb R^2. If zk,j→zjz_{k,j}\to z_j for every jj, then Pk→P:=conv⁡{z1,…,zN}P_k\longrightarrow P:=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{z_1,\ldots,z_N\} in the Hausdorff metric. If PP has nonempty interior, then area⁡(Pk)→area⁡(P)\operatorname{area}(P_k)\to\operatorname{area}(P). Moreover, for every fixed linear map AA, the conditions APk⊆PkAP_k\subseteq P_k pass to the limit, and maxz∈Pk|z|→maxz∈P|z|.\max_{z\in P_k}|z|\longrightarrow\max_{z\in P}|z|.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. For nonempty compact sets K,L⊆ℝ2K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^2, their Hausdorff distance is dH(K,L)=max⁡{supx∈Kdist(x,L),supy∈Ldist(y,K)},d_H(K,L)=\max\left\{ \sup_{x\in K}\operatorname{dist}(x,L), \sup_{y\in L}\operatorname{dist}(y,K) \right\}, and we write 𝔻¯={z∈ℝ2:|z|≤1}\overline{\mathbb D}=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z|\leq1\} for the closed unit disk.

    Put εk=max⁡j|zk,j−zj|\varepsilon_k=\max_j|z_{k,j}-z_j|. If z=∑jtjzk,j∈Pkz=\sum_jt_jz_{k,j}\in P_k, then z′=∑jtjzj∈Pz'=\sum_jt_jz_j\in P and |z−z′|≤εk|z-z'|\leq\varepsilon_k; the converse estimate is identical. Hence the Hausdorff distance is at most εk\varepsilon_k.

    All polygons lie in one large disk. We prove convergence of their indicator functions away from ∂P\partial P. If z∉Pz\notin P, its positive distance from PP and Hausdorff convergence imply z∉Pkz\notin P_k for large kk. If z∈int⁡(P)z\in\operatorname{int}(P), choose r>0r>0 with z+r𝔻¯⊆Pz+r\overline{\mathbb D}\subseteq P. Hausdorff convergence implies uniform convergence of support functions: |hPk(u)−hP(u)|≤dH(Pk,P)(|u|=1).|h_{P_k}(u)-h_P(u)|\leq d_H(P_k,P) \qquad(|u|=1). The contained disk gives the uniform support margin hP(u)≥⟨u,z⟩+r(|u|=1).h_P(u)\geq\langle u,z\rangle+r \qquad(|u|=1). For large kk, when dH(Pk,P)<rd_H(P_k,P)<r, these two estimates give hPk(u)>⟨u,z⟩(|u|=1).h_{P_k}(u)>\langle u,z\rangle \qquad(|u|=1). If z∉Pkz\notin P_k, Lemma A.2 would produce a unit vector uu with ⟨u,z⟩>hPk(u)\langle u,z\rangle>h_{P_k}(u), a contradiction. Thus z∈Pkz\in P_k eventually. All generating points lie in one compact set, so one fixed disk R𝔻¯R\overline{\mathbb D} contains every PkP_k and PP. The indicators of the polygons are therefore dominated by the integrable function 𝟏R𝔻¯\mathbf 1_{R\overline{\mathbb D}}. Since the boundary of a polygon is a finite union of line segments and has planar measure zero, dominated convergence of the indicator functions gives area convergence.

    If APk⊆PkAP_k\subseteq P_k for every kk and x∈Px\in P, choose xk∈Pkx_k\in P_k with xk→xx_k\to x. Then Axk∈PkAx_k\in P_k, and Hausdorff convergence gives Ax∈PAx\in P. Finally, by convexity of the norm, maxz∈Pk|z|=maxj|zk,j|,maxz∈P|z|=maxj|zj|,\max_{z\in P_k}|z|=\max_j|z_{k,j}|, \qquad \max_{z\in P}|z|=\max_j|z_j|, which proves convergence of the maxima. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Match convex combinations
      Replacing every labelled generator by its limit moves any convex combination by at most the largest generator displacement. Repeat in reverse.
    2. 2
      Control exterior points
      A point outside compact P has positive distance from it, so Hausdorff-near polygons eventually miss that point.
    3. 3
      Control interior points
      An interior disk supplies a fixed positive margin in every support direction. If the point stayed outside an approximating polygon, strict separation would contradict that margin.
    4. 4
      Pass area to the limit
      Indicator functions converge away from the finite union of boundary segments, a set of area zero; dominated convergence applies inside one common disk.
    5. 5
      Pass invariance and radius
      Approximate each limit point by points in the polygons to preserve AP⊆P; maximal radius reduces to the maximum of finitely many generator norms.

    Key conclusionFinite vertex convergence is strong enough to produce the limiting polygon and preserve its area, maximal radius, and invariance under the fixed map.

  2. A.5
    Lemma A.5Classical result

    Strict area monotonicity

    Show that proper inclusion of two full-dimensional planar convex bodies creates a strictly positive area gain.

    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionSeparate one new point of the larger body from the smaller body. Moving a small interior disk toward that point produces a smaller disk of positive area inside the larger body but outside the smaller one.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma A.5 (Strict area monotonicity). If K⊆LK\subseteq L are compact convex subsets of ℝ2\mathbb R^2, both with nonempty interior, and K≠LK\neq L, then area⁡(K)<area⁡(L).\operatorname{area}(K)<\operatorname{area}(L).

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. Choose y∈L\Ky\in L\setminus K. By Lemma A.2, there is a linear functional ℓ\ell with ℓ(y)>a:=maxx∈Kℓ(x).\ell(y)>a:=\max_{x\in K}\ell(x). Choose a closed disk D⊂int⁡(K)D\subset\operatorname{int}(K). The convex hull C=conv⁡(D∪{y})C=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}(D\cup\{y\}) satisfies C⊆LC\subseteq L. Because D⊂int⁡(K)D\subset\operatorname{int}(K), compactness gives max⁡Dℓ<a<ℓ(y)\max_D\ell<a<\ell(y). Choose t∈(0,1)t\in(0,1) sufficiently close to 11 that (1−t)minDℓ+tℓ(y)>a.(1-t)\min_D\ell+t\ell(y)>a. Then Dt=(1−t)D+tyD_t=(1-t)D+ty is a disk of positive area contained in CC, and every x∈Dtx\in D_t satisfies ℓ(x)>a\ell(x)>a. Hence Dt⊆L\KD_t\subseteq L\setminus K, so L\KL\setminus K has positive area. This proves the strict inequality. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Choose and separate a new point
      Take y∈L\K and a functional whose value at y is strictly above its maximum on K.
    2. 2
      Choose an interior disk
      A closed disk inside K has positive width in every direction.
    3. 3
      Move the disk beyond the support level
      For a parameter t sufficiently close to 1, the homothetic image Dt=(1−t)D+t y is a disk of positive area contained in L and strictly beyond the maximum of the separating functional on K.
    4. 4
      Compare areas
      The disk Dt lies in L∖K and has positive area, so the proper inclusion increases area strictly.

    Key conclusionA proper convex clip that preserves nonempty interior must reduce area strictly.

    Classification and sourcesWhy this result carries its displayed label

    Classical result. Schneider, Chapter 1, §§1.1 and 1.3, supplies strict separation and convex-body monotonicity; the proof here exhibits an explicit positive-area disk inside L∖K.

  3. 4.9
    Lemma 4.9Previously known

    Boundary-arc bound for a least-area normalized polygon

    Choose a least-area normalized invariant N-gon and bound how many old vertices can lie on each discarded boundary arc.

    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Unit maximal-radius normalization about the origin
    Rescale the polygon so maxz∈P|z|=1. The centre is fixed at the origin; this is not the movable-centre circumradius used elsewhere in geometry. Without a fixed scale, every polygon could be shrunk and “least area” would be meaningless.
    Area-minimizer
    A normalized invariant polygon with exactly N vertices whose area is no larger than that of any other normalized invariant polygon with exactly N vertices. Lemma 4.9 proves existence by combining finite-dimensional compactness, the limit properties in Lemma A.4, and continuity of area.
    Proper cut
    A clipping for which Pj is a proper subset of P, so a nonempty two-dimensional region is actually removed.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionIf a discarded arc contains three old vertices, clipping produces an invariant polygon with too few vertices. If it contains two, clipping produces a smaller normalized invariant N-gon unless the open arc contains the chosen vertex v with |v|=1.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.9 (Old-vertex bound on discarded boundary arcs). Let T(z)=λzT(z)=\lambda z, where 0<|λ|<10<|\lambda|<1 and λ∉ℝ\lambda\notin\mathbb R, and assume that TT is NN-critical. Among the NN-gons satisfying λP⊆P\lambda P\subseteq P, impose the unit maximal-radius normalization maxz∈P|z|=1.\begin{equation} \label{eq:maximal-radius-normalization} \max_{z\in P}|z|=1. \end{equation}(4.3) The normalized class is nonempty and contains a member of least area; choose such a polygon PP. Let Q=λPQ=\lambda P and use the notation of Lemma 4.8. Then every proper image-edge cut, meaning Pj≠PP_j\neq P, satisfies kj≤2,\begin{equation} \label{eq:k-at-most-two} k_j\leq2, \end{equation}(4.4) and at most one proper image-edge cut has kj=2k_j=2.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. Because νpoly(T)=N\nu_{\mathrm{poly}}(T)=N, an invariant polygon with exactly NN extreme vertices exists. Its maximal distance from the origin is positive, and rescaling it to make that maximum equal to 11 preserves both invariance and the number of extreme vertices. Thus the normalized class is nonempty. Write 𝔻¯={z∈ℝ2:|z|≤1}.\overline{\mathbb D}=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z|\leq1\}.

    We next prove that the normalized class is compact in the Hausdorff metric. Take an arbitrary sequence in this class and display each member as the convex hull of its NN vertices, all of which lie in 𝔻¯\overline{\mathbb D}. Compactness of (𝔻¯)N(\overline{\mathbb D})^N gives a subsequence for which the NN-tuples converge. By Lemma A.4, their convex hulls converge in the Hausdorff metric to a convex hull P∞P_\infty with at most NN extreme points; the same lemma preserves the maximal radius and the inclusion λP∞⊆P∞\lambda P_\infty\subseteq P_\infty.

    The limit cannot be a singleton: if P∞={v}P_\infty=\{v\}, normalization gives |v|=1|v|=1, while invariance gives λv=v\lambda v=v, impossible because 0<|λ|<10<|\lambda|<1. It cannot be a nondegenerate segment either. Indeed, if a≠ba\neq b are its endpoints, then λa,λb∈P∞\lambda a,\lambda b\in P_\infty, so the nonzero vectors b−ab-a and λ(b−a)\lambda(b-a) are parallel, which is impossible for nonreal λ\lambda. Thus P∞P_\infty has nonempty interior. If it had fewer than NN extreme points, it would be an invariant polygon with fewer than NN vertices, contrary to νpoly(T)=N\nu_{\mathrm{poly}}(T)=N. Hence it has exactly NN extreme points and belongs to the normalized class. We have shown that every sequence has a subsequence converging within the class, so the class is compact. Area is continuous by Lemma A.4; therefore a least-area normalized representative exists.

    By Theorem 3.2, every vertex of Q=λPQ=\lambda P lies on ∂P\partial P. Thus all hypotheses of Lemma 4.8 hold for this least-area polygon, and its clipping conclusions apply.

    Every point of PP is a convex combination of its vertices, so convexity of the norm gives maxz∈P|z|=maxx∈Ext⁡(P)|x|.\max_{z\in P}|z|=\max_{x\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P)}|x|. Choose a vertex vv with |v|=1|v|=1. Since Q⊆ρ𝔻¯Q\subseteq\rho\overline{\mathbb D}, where ρ=|λ|<1\rho=|\lambda|<1, the point vv is not a vertex of QQ. If a proper image-edge cut had kj≥3k_j\geq3, then PjP_j would contain the full-dimensional polygon QQ, would satisfy λPj⊆Pj\lambda P_j\subseteq P_j, and would have at most N−1N-1 vertices by (4.2). This contradicts νpoly(T)=N\nu_{\mathrm{poly}}(T)=N.

    Suppose a proper image-edge cut has kj=2k_j=2 and v∉Aj∘v\notin A_j^\circ. By the discarded-boundary equality in Lemma 4.8, v∈Pjv\in P_j. Hence PjP_j remains normalized. It contains the full-dimensional polygon QQ, is invariant, and has at most NN vertices. Criticality rules out fewer than NN vertices, so PjP_j has exactly NN extreme vertices and is another invariant NN-gon. But Pj⊊PP_j\subsetneq P and both polygons have nonempty interior, so Lemma A.5 gives area⁡(Pj)<area⁡(P)\operatorname{area}(P_j)<\operatorname{area}(P). This contradicts the choice of PP.

    The open positive arcs Aj∘=(yj,yj+1)∂PA_j^\circ=(y_j,y_{j+1})_{\partial P} are pairwise disjoint and together cover ∂P\Ext⁡(Q)\partial P\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(Q). Since vv is not a QQ-vertex, it belongs to exactly one of them. Therefore at most one proper image-edge cut with kj=2k_j=2 can escape the preceding contradiction. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Extract a convergent subsequence
      Write each polygon as the hull of N points in the closed unit disk, allow repetitions, and take a convergent subsequence of these N-tuples.
    2. 2
      Preserve the defining conditions
      Lemma A.4 preserves the hull, invariance, maximal radius one, and area. A singleton would force λv=v; a segment would require an invariant real direction. Both are impossible for a nonreal contraction.
    3. 3
      Use criticality at the limit
      Fewer than N extreme points would contradict νpoly(T)=N. Hence the limit is an invariant N-gon whose displayed vertices are exactly its extreme points, and it attains least area.
    4. 4
      Exclude arcs with three old vertices
      The clipping count would give an invariant polygon with at most N−1 vertices, contradicting νpoly(T)=N.
    5. 5
      Exclude ordinary two-vertex arcs
      If the open discarded arc misses a maximal-radius vertex v, the clipped polygon remains normalized. Criticality forces it to have exactly N vertices, while Lemma A.5 gives it strictly smaller area.
    6. 6
      Count the sole exception
      Because Q lies in the disk of radius |λ|<1, v is not a vertex of Q. The open discarded arcs are pairwise disjoint, so v belongs to at most one of them. Only that arc escapes the area comparison.

    Key conclusionEvery proper cut discards an arc containing at most two old vertices, and at most one discarded arc can contain two.
