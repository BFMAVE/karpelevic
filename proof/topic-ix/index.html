Topic IX · Manuscript pages 76–82

Candidate curves from the Ito equation on Farey intervals

How does the Ito equation determine a unique modulus at each prescribed argument between consecutive Farey fractions?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

Topic VIII defined the Karpelevič region Θₙ from row-stochastic matrices. Topic IX fixes two consecutive Farey fractions and, at each prescribed unit-circle argument between them, uses a strictly increasing real equation to determine one modulus and connect the resulting point to the reduced Ito polynomial.

Endpoint limits and the additional segment [−1,−1/2] for n=3 are proved before these points are assembled into a compact candidate arc over the closed Farey interval. Stochastic realization and identification with the boundary of Θₙ are deliberately deferred.

Reading convention. Essential definitions appear before the first statement that needs them. Complete manuscript proofs are closed by default. When a result has an added line-by-line explanation, it appears inside the same disclosure as the proof.

Definitions and parameter range

From the Karpelevič region to consecutive Farey fractions

A matrix P=(pᵢⱼ) is row-stochastic when pᵢⱼ≥0 and Σⱼpᵢⱼ=1 for every row i. As in Topic VIII, Θₙ is the set of all complex eigenvalues of n×n row-stochastic matrices.

This is Topic IX of the online proof reader, whereas the theorem and equation numbers retain their labels from §II.2 of the manuscript. The two numbering systems are independent.

For t∈[0,1/2], the point exp(2πit) runs once along the upper unit semicircle. At a prescribed parameter x, the ray at argument 2πx is {ρ exp(2πix):ρ≥0}. Fix n≥3. The Farey sequence Fₙ contains every reduced fraction in [0,1] whose denominator is at most n, in increasing order. We restrict to Fₙ⁺=Fₙ∩[0,1/2]; the superscript + refers to the upper semicircle under t↦exp(2πit). Two consecutive fractions f<g in Fₙ form a Farey interval [f,g]. When both fractions lie in [0,1/2], they are equivalently consecutive in Fₙ⁺. Fractions are always reduced and denominators are positive.

IX.A

Criterion for consecutive Farey fractions

The determinant and denominator criterion is proved before either consequence is used in the angular coordinates.

  1. II.2.1
    Lemma II.2.1Classical result

    Criterion for consecutive Farey fractions

    Recognizes consecutive fractions using only a determinant and a denominator bound.

    Definitions and notation used here4 entries to consult as needed
    Reduced fraction
    A fraction a/b with b>0 whose numerator and denominator have greatest common divisor one.
    Open an example
    The rational number 2/6 appears in a Farey sequence as the reduced fraction 1/3.
    Farey sequence Fₙ
    All reduced fractions in [0,1] with denominator at most n, written in increasing order.
    Primitive lattice vector
    An integer vector whose coordinates have greatest common divisor one. The reduced fraction a/b corresponds to the primitive vector (b,a).
    Mediant
    The fraction (a+c)/(b+d), which lies strictly between a/b and c/d.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionDeterminant one means the primitive endpoint vectors form a basis of the integer lattice. The condition b+d>n says even the mediant has denominator too large for Fₙ.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma II.2.1 (Criterion for consecutive Farey fractions). Two reduced fractions a0/b0<a1/b1a_0/b_0<a_1/b_1 belonging to FnF_n are consecutive in FnF_n if and only if b0a1−a0b1=1,b0+b1>n.\begin{equation} \label{karp:eq:farey-neighbor} b_0a_1-a_0b_1=1, \qquad b_0+b_1>n. \end{equation}(II.2.1)

      Encode an intermediate fraction
      For a/b<h/k<c/d, the positive integers m=ck−dh and ℓ=bh−ak are the two determinants obtained from the endpoint and intermediate primitive vectors.
    2. 2

    3. 3

      If b+d>n, every intermediate reduced fraction has denominator larger than n.
    4. 4

      Topic V’s lattice-parallelogram lemma supplies a nonzero integer point strictly inside the fundamental parallelogram spanned by the endpoint vectors.
    5. 5

      That point and its complementary representative have slopes strictly between the endpoints; one has first coordinate at most (b+d)/2≤n.
    6. 6

      If b+d≤n, the reduced mediant lies in Fₙ between the endpoints. Both necessary conditions follow.

Arithmetic coordinates

Coordinates and angle bounds on a fixed Farey interval

Fix consecutive fractions f<g in Fₙ⁺. Lemma II.2.1 shows that their determinant has absolute value one. In particular their denominators are unequal, so label the endpoint with smaller denominator p/q and the other r/s, with q<s:

p/q, r/s, q<s. (II.2.2)

These labels need not follow the left-to-right order f<g.

Choose x∈(f,g) and set

d=⌊n/q⌋, e=s−dq. (II.2.3)

The signed integer e is retained; it may be positive, zero, or negative. The positive angular distances used by the scalar equation are

A=2π|qx−p|, B=(2π/d)|sx−r|. (II.2.6)

These angles have the required signs for an elementary reason. Set u=|x−p/q|/|r/s−p/q|∈(0,1). Consecutiveness gives |rq−ps|=1, hence |qx−p|=u/s and |sx−r|=(1−u)/q. Therefore (A+B)/(2π)=u/s+(1−u)/(dq)<1/2 because s≥3 and dq≥2. Indeed, Lemma II.2.1 gives q+s>n≥3; together with q<s, this excludes s≤2. Also d=⌊n/q⌋≥1; if q≥2 then dq≥2, while if q=1 then dq=n≥3. Since u∈(0,1), the displayed convex combination is strictly below one half.

0<A,B,A+B<π. (II.2.7)

Consequently every sine factor used below is positive.

IX.B

The Ito polynomial family

With the Farey data and angle bounds now established, the Ito equation defines the relevant family of polynomials. The equation alone does not select the continuous root used below.

    Associates one polynomial family with each interval between consecutive Farey fractions.

The radial equation

A unique modulus at the prescribed argument

For the fixed Farey interval and prescribed argument, define Fₓ(ρ)=ρˢ⁄ᵈ sin A+ρᑫ sin B−sin(A+B) on [0,1]. Both exponents and both coefficients of the powers of ρ are positive, so Fₓ is strictly increasing. Proposition II.2.3 proves Fₓ(0)<0<Fₓ(1); consequently it has exactly one zero ρ(x)∈(0,1).

The associated point is λ=ρ exp(2πix). The proof constructs positive coefficients α and β with α+β=1, defines the required fractional power by an explicit exponential, and derives the displayed vector equation. No phrase such as “take the principal root” is left implicit.

IX.C

Unique modulus, endpoint limits, and the candidate curve

The next statements prove existence, uniqueness, algebraic satisfaction of the Ito equation, endpoint continuity, and the exact segment [−1,−1/2] in the case n=3 before the set Γf,g(n) receives its formal definition.

  1. II.2.3

    Proves exact existence and uniqueness of the modulus at each argument in the open Farey interval, without presupposing a polynomial root branch or using numerical approximation.

    Proposition II.2.4

    Endpoint limits, including the case n=3

    Determines the endpoint limits and hence the closure of each parametrized arc, including the one case where the nonreal graph does not reach its unit-circle endpoint.

    Definitions and notation used here2 entries to consult as needed
    Subsequential limit
    A limit obtained after selecting a convergent subsequence. If every convergent subsequence has the same limit, the original bounded sequence converges to it.
    Implicit-function theorem
    A calculus theorem that gives a continuously differentiable local solution when the defining function is continuously differentiable and its derivative in the solved-for variable does not vanish. A direct sequential continuity proof is also provided here.

    Complete manuscript statement

IX.D

Certified numerical evaluation

For an exact prescribed argument, the procedure first certifies its Farey interval. It then uses bisection on the strictly increasing residual, retaining a rigorous error bound for the returned modulus.

  1. II.2.6
    Algorithm II.2.6

    Certified numerical evaluation

    Certifies an enclosure of the exact modulus from Proposition II.2.3 and returns an ε-certified polar point approximation or, only when n=3 and x=1/2, the exact interval [−1,−1/2].

The interval computation below does not establish or select a root; it certifies an enclosure of that exact modulus. For an interior argument, maintain a sign-certified bracket [L,U]. When U−L≤2ε, return its midpoint; if an exact zero is certified, return m immediately.
