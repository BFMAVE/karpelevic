Topic IV · Manuscript pages 19–30

From endpoint order to one interval of relative-interior contacts

How does a geometric contact become a permitted update of a finite cyclic index set?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

The boundary-interval bound from Topic III and a finite endpoint count select one consistent half-open orientation. Cyclic order turns the global contact assignment into the side-label translation Eᵢ↦Eᵢ₊κ, while lifted angles encode its shift κ without hidden multiples of 2π.

An exact vertex replacement then realizes one permitted update of the subset S, and finite induction realizes every permitted finite update sequence. Among the sets obtained by finitely many such updates, a lexicographically minimal one reduces to one cyclic interval. Source notes identify Karpelevič’s antecedent arguments and state precisely what is proved on this page.

The proof in four steps

From boundary order to one interval

  1. Choose one half-open convention. Endpoint counts and cyclic order place exactly one image vertex in every half-open side.
  2. Identify one cyclic shift. The side assignment is order preserving, so under the chosen labels it is the translation Ei↦Ei+κ; lifted angles locate κ without an unaccounted multiple of 2π.
  3. Realize one index-set update. Proposition 5.1 replaces one polygon vertex and proves the exact change S↦(S∖{i})∪{i+κ}.
  4. Minimize among realizable sets. Call a set reachable when it is obtained from the initial S by finitely many updates realized by Proposition 5.1. Among these reachable sets, one consists of a single cyclic interval.

IV-A · Endpoint order

A finite endpoint count chooses the right-half-open endpoint convention. Cyclic order then turns the contact assignment into the side-label translation Ei↦Ei+κ, and lifted angles encode it without an unaccounted multiple of 2π.

  1. 4.10
    Lemma 4.10

    Finite cyclic endpoint count

    Convert one possible arc containing two vertices and one possible arc containing none into a uniform count for the opposite half-open convention.

    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Cyclic word
    A finite list whose last entry is followed again by its first; every index is read modulo N.
    Binary transition
    A change 0→1 or 1→0 between adjacent entries, including the wrap from entry N−1 back to entry 0.
    Changing the included endpoint
    Adding cj−cj+1 to the right-half-open count removes the old included endpoint when necessary and includes the opposite endpoint instead.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionIf one of N counts is 2 while their total remains N, exactly one count must be 0. A cyclic binary word has equally many upward and downward transitions, so changing which endpoint is included cancels these two exceptional counts.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.10 (Finite cyclic endpoint count). Let (rj)j∈ℤ/Nℤ(r_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z} take values in {0,1,2}\{0,1,2\}, satisfy ∑jrj=N\sum_jr_j=N, and contain at most one entry equal to 22.     Proof. If no entry is 22, then every rj≤1r_j\leq1 and the sum of the NN entries is NN, so all entries equal 11. Suppose rs=2r_s=2. Its surplus over 11 is one. Since no second entry can exceed 11, the equality ∑jrj=N\sum_jr_j=N forces a unique zero rt=0r_t=0 and makes every remaining entry equal to 11.

    The transition 0→10\to1 at ss in (4.5) forces at least one transition 1→01\to0 in the cyclic binary word. By hypothesis it can occur only at the unique zero index tt. In a cyclic binary word the number of 0→10\to1 transitions equals the number of 1→01\to0 transitions, because ∑j(cj+1−cj)=0.\sum_j(c_{j+1}-c_j)=0. Consequently the transitions at ss and tt are the unique transitions of their respective types.     Guided proof

      Handle the uniform case
      Without an entry 2, all N entries are at most 1 and sum to N, so every entry is 1.
    2. 2
      Locate the unique deficit

    3. 3

    4. 4

    5. 5

    Lemma 4.11

    Cyclic interlacing with endpoint membership

    Upgrade Topic III’s boundary-interval bound into a global assertion: one consistently oriented half-open gap contains exactly one vertex of P everywhere.

    Recall from Topic III

    The boundary objects and estimates used below

    Q and yj
    Q=λP is the image polygon, and the yj are its vertices in positive cyclic order.
    kj
    The number of vertices of P on the closed positive boundary interval from yj to yj+1.
    Supporting image-edge line

    Proper image-edge cut

    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Interlacing

    Complete manuscript statement

    Order-preserving half-open contact assignment

    Show that every vertex-to-side contact label is one fixed cyclic shift and place the argument of the multiplier in its corresponding rational interval.

    Definitions and notation used here4 entries to consult as needed
    Cyclic-order-preserving bijection
    A bijection sending each successor to the successor of its image. Once the image of one index is known, all others follow, so the map is a single translation modulo N.
    Integer representative κ
    The cyclic shift is initially defined modulo N. The choice κ∈{1,…,N} is the representative compatible with the positive argument of the multiplier.
    Lifted polar angle
    A real angle Θi, not just an angle modulo 2π, chosen with Θi+N=Θi+2π.
    The integer mᵢ

      An added side index already in S lowers |S|; a disjoint translate adjacent to another component lowers the component count; a disjoint nonadjacent translate preserves both entries. Minimality permits only the third outcome.
    4. 4
      Make the successive updates repeatable
      After each complete update, the translated interval is again a connected component of CN[S], disjoint from and nonadjacent to every unchanged component. Induction therefore permits arbitrarily many complete translates.
    5. 5
      Force every orbit of addition by κ to meet S
      If one orbit of j↦j+κ contained only endpoint contacts, iterating their exact identities around its length N/δ would give xj=λN/δxj, impossible for a nonzero vertex and |λ|<1.
    6. 6
      Separate residues of distinct components
      If two components contained indices congruent modulo δ, repeated complete updates would send an index of one component to an index of the other, or would improve the lexicographic pair earlier. Both contradict minimality.
    7. 7
      Force one connected component
      A component of length at least δ already meets every residue class modulo δ. If all components are shorter, their disjoint intervals in the cyclic order of these residue classes cover every class; suitable κ-translates make two consecutive intervals adjacent, decreasing the component count.
    8. 8
      Bound the cyclic interval length
      The unique component must meet every one of the δ residues, so after cyclic relabelling it is {1,…,φ}, where φ=|S|≥δ.
    9. 9
      Extract the first-entrance identity without Topic V
      When φ<N, update only the terminal index. Before its first entry into {N−φ,…,N−1}, its following index is outside S; entering the fixed part above its left endpoint would decrease cardinality. Hence the first entry is exactly N−φ, with every earlier residue smaller. The case φ=N uses the separate initial value h=0.

    ConclusionThe reachable set S has been reduced to one cyclic interval, together with one exact first-entrance relation for addition by κ modulo N.
