Topic IV · Manuscript pages 19–30

From endpoint order to contact reduction

How does a geometric contact become a legal move on a finite cyclic board?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

The boundary-interval bound from Topic III and a finite endpoint count select one consistent half-open orientation. Cyclic order turns the global contact assignment into a single shift κ, while lifted angles record that shift without hidden multiples of 2π.

An exact corner surgery then realizes one legal Boolean move, and finite induction realizes every legal sweep. A lexicographically minimal reachable contact pattern reduces to one strict block. Karpelevič is cited as a historical antecedent where relevant, but an occurrence there alone is not treated as proof that the exact result was previously established.

Reading convention. Essential definitions appear before the first statement that needs them. Complete manuscript proofs are closed by default. When a result has an added line-by-line explanation, it appears inside the same disclosure as the proof.

  1. 4.10
    Lemma 4.10Previously known

    Finite cyclic endpoint count

    Convert one possible overfull boundary gap and one possible empty gap into a uniform opposite half-open count.

    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Cyclic word
    A finite list whose last entry is followed again by its first; every index is read modulo N.
    Binary transition
    A change 0→1 or 1→0 between adjacent entries, including the wrap from entry N−1 back to entry 0.
    Endpoint correction
    cj−cj+1 changes a right-half-open count into the opposite left-half-open count.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionIf one of N counts is 2 while their total remains N, exactly one count must be 0. A cyclic binary word has equally many upward and downward transitions, so the two exceptional counts cancel after the endpoint correction.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.10 (Finite cyclic endpoint count). Let (rj)j∈ℤ/Nℤ(r_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z} take values in {0,1,2}\{0,1,2\}, satisfy ∑jrj=N\sum_jr_j=N, and contain at most one entry equal to 22. Let (cj)j∈ℤ/Nℤ(c_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z} be a binary cyclic word. If an entry rs=2r_s=2 occurs, assume cs=0,cs+1=1,\begin{equation} \label{eq:cyclic-ledger-up-transition} c_s=0, \qquad c_{s+1}=1, \end{equation}(4.5) and assume that every transition cj=1c_j=1, cj+1=0c_{j+1}=0 can occur only at an index jj for which rj=0r_j=0. Then either rj=1r_j=1 for every jj, or there are unique indices s,ts,t such that rs=2,rt=0,rj=1(j∉{s,t}),r_s=2, \qquad r_t=0, \qquad r_j=1\quad(j\notin\{s,t\}), and in the latter case ℓj:=rj+cj−cj+1=1(j∈ℤ/Nℤ).\begin{equation} \label{eq:opposite-gap-count-ledger} \ell_j:=r_j+c_j-c_{j+1}=1 \qquad(j\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z). \end{equation}(4.6)

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. If no entry is 22, then every rj≤1r_j\leq1 and the sum of the NN entries is NN, so all entries equal 11. Suppose rs=2r_s=2. Its surplus over 11 is one. Since no second entry can exceed 11, the equality ∑jrj=N\sum_jr_j=N forces a unique zero rt=0r_t=0 and makes every remaining entry equal to 11.

    The transition 0→10\to1 at ss in (4.5) forces at least one transition 1→01\to0 in the cyclic binary word. By hypothesis it can occur only at the unique zero index tt. In a cyclic binary word the number of 0→10\to1 transitions equals the number of 1→01\to0 transitions, because ∑j(cj+1−cj)=0.\sum_j(c_{j+1}-c_j)=0. Consequently the transitions at ss and tt are the unique transitions of their respective types. Hence cj=cj+1c_j=c_{j+1} for j∉{s,t}j\notin\{s,t\}, while (cs,cs+1)=(0,1),(ct,ct+1)=(1,0).(c_s,c_{s+1})=(0,1), \qquad (c_t,c_{t+1})=(1,0). Substitution gives ℓs=2+0−1=1\ell_s=2+0-1=1, ℓt=0+1−0=1\ell_t=0+1-0=1, and ℓj=1\ell_j=1 at every remaining index. ◻

    Lemma 4.11Previously known

    Cyclic interlacing with endpoint bookkeeping

    Upgrade Topic III’s boundary-interval bound into a global assertion: one consistently oriented half-open gap contains exactly one outer vertex everywhere.

    Recall from Topic III

    The boundary objects used below

    Q and yj
    Q=λP is the image polygon, and the yj are its vertices in positive cyclic order.
    kj
    The number of vertices of P on the closed positive boundary interval from yj to yj+1.
    Two kinds of image edge
    If [yj,yj+1] lies on one side of P, no part of P is removed. Otherwise its line makes a proper cut and removes a boundary interval. Topic III proves the required bounds in both cases.
    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Interlacing
    Two cyclic point sets interlace when exactly one point of one set occurs in each specified gap between consecutive points of the other.
    Orientation reversal
    Complex conjugation reflects the plane and reverses boundary order. Reindexing by x̃i=conj(x−i) restores positive order.
    Closed-interval count versus half-open count
    kj counts vertices on the closed positive boundary interval; rj counts them on the corresponding right-half-open interval. Their difference is whether the left endpoint is already a polygon vertex.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionTopic III allows at most one gap of size 2. If it occurs, the total creates one gap of size 0. Side touching dictates where endpoint status can change, exactly matching Lemma 4.10’s finite count.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 4.11 (Cyclic interlacing with endpoint bookkeeping). Let PP be the least-area representative of Lemma 4.9. Then one of the following two alternatives holds:

    1. every arc (yj,yj+1]∂P(y_j,y_{j+1}]_{\partial P} contains exactly one vertex of PP;

    2. every arc [yj,yj+1)∂P[y_j,y_{j+1})_{\partial P} contains exactly one vertex of PP.

    In alternative (ii), every right-half-open side of PP contains exactly one vertex of QQ. Thus a right-half-open convention is obtained without ever reading a convex polygon in the wrong orientation.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. Put cj={1,yj∈Ext⁡(P),0,yj∉Ext⁡(P),rj=#(Ext⁡(P)∩(yj,yj+1]∂P).c_j=\begin{cases}1,&y_j\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P),\\0,&y_j\notin\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P),\end{cases} \qquad r_j=\#\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P)\cap(y_j,y_{j+1}]_{\partial P}\bigr).     If no rjr_j equals 22, the combination of (4.8) and (4.10) forces rj=1r_j=1 for every jj, which is alternative (i). Otherwise there are unique indices s,ts,t with rs=2,rt=0,rj=1(j≠s,t).\begin{equation} \label{eq:two-zero-pattern} r_s=2, \qquad r_t=0, \qquad r_j=1\quad(j\neq s,t). \end{equation}(4.11) We determine the endpoint statuses at the two exceptional arcs.

    First, cs=0c_s=0 by (4.9). Also cs+1=1c_{s+1}=1. For if ys+1y_{s+1} were not a PP-vertex, the two PP-vertices in the open gap would be consecutive vertices of PP, and their joining side would contain no QQ-vertex. This contradicts Lemma 4.1 and full side touching. Thus the binary cyclic word (cj)(c_j) has a transition 0→10\to1 at ss.

    Now consider a transition cj=1c_j=1, cj+1=0c_{j+1}=0 with j≠tj\neq t. Then rj=1r_j=1, so (4.9) gives kj=2k_j=2. The image-edge cut is proper. Indeed, if its line also supported PP, the positive arc starts at the PP-vertex yjy_j and ends at a nonvertex yj+1y_{j+1}, so the arc must stop before the next PP-vertex, so it would have rj=0r_j=0, contrary to rj=1r_j=1. Therefore the opposite half-open counts ℓj=#(Ext⁡(P)∩[yj,yj+1)∂P)=rj+cj−cj+1\ell_j=\#\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}(P)\cap[y_j,y_{j+1})_{\partial P}\bigr) =r_j+c_j-c_{j+1} all equal 11.     In alternative (i), let xjx_j be the unique vertex in (yj,yj+1](y_j,y_{j+1}]. Then yj+1∈[xj,xj+1)y_{j+1}\in[x_j,x_{j+1}). Conjugation is a bijection on vertices and takes QQ to Q∼=λ¯P∼\widetilde Q=\overline\lambda\,\widetilde P, so every such side contains exactly one vertex of Q∼\widetilde Q. This proves the final assertion with all endpoint inclusions explicit. ◻

    Corollary 4.12Previously known

    One image vertex in every half-open side

    Turn interlacing into a labelled bijection between the image vertices and the half-open outer sides.

    Definitions and notation used here1 entry to consult as needed
    Collision-free
    No image vertex is assigned to two side indices, and no side index receives two image vertices.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionEvery side receives at least one image vertex. There are exactly N sides and N image vertices, while disjoint half-open sides prevent duplicate assignment; finite counting forces a bijection.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Corollary 4.12 (Collision-free global half-open assignment). In either orientation supplied by Lemma 4.11, the contact assignment is a genuine labeled bijection {vertices of Q}↔{Ei+:i∈ℤ/Nℤ}.\{\text{vertices of }Q\}\longleftrightarrow \{E_i^+:i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\}. Equivalently, no image vertex can be assigned to two sides and no side can receive two image vertices. After the possible conjugation in (4.7), the labels may be chosen so that the unique image assigned to Ei+E_i^+ is denoted by ξi\xi_i.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. The half-open sides Ei+=(xi−1,xi]E_i^+=(x_{i-1},x_i] are pairwise disjoint and their union is ∂P\partial P. In alternative (ii) of Lemma 4.11, each interval [yj,yj+1)∂P[y_j,y_{j+1})_{\partial P} contains exactly one vertex of PP, and the proof there gives yj∈Ej+y_j\in E_j^+. Thus every side contains at least one image vertex. There are exactly NN sides and exactly NN image vertices, while the disjoint half-open partition prevents one image vertex from belonging to two sides; hence the assignment is a bijection. In alternative (i), the same argument applies after the conjugate reindexing (4.7). This proves the stated collision-free labeling. ◻

    Lemma 4.13Previously known

    One-sided contact representative

    Show that every source-to-side contact label is one fixed cyclic shift and trap the multiplier angle in its corresponding rational interval.

    Definitions and notation used here5 entries to consult as needed
    Cyclic-order-preserving bijection
    A bijection sending each successor to the successor of its image. Once the image of one index is known, all others follow, so the map is a single translation modulo N.
    Integer lift κ
    The cyclic shift is initially defined modulo N. Choosing κ∈{1,…,N} records the representative compatible with the positive multiplier angle.
    Greatest common divisor
    gcd(N,κ) is the largest positive integer dividing both N and κ. Repeated addition of κ modulo N returns after exactly N/gcd(N,κ) steps.
    Lifted polar angle
    A real angle Θi, not just an angle modulo 2π, chosen with Θi+N=Θi+2π.
    Winding integer
    The possible multiple 2πmi introduced before the correct lift of a cyclic angle relation has been identified.
