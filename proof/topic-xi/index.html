Topic XI · Manuscript pages 91–93

Explicit stochastic realization of the candidate curve

How can every candidate point be realized by an actual stochastic matrix?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

A sparse realization graph turns the reduced Ito polynomial into the characteristic polynomial of a row-stochastic matrix. The proof derives the determinant expansion over pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles and classifies every directed cycle in the graph.

This independently realizes every point on the Topic IX candidate curve. Only after that reverse inclusion is established does the chapter combine it with Topic X and activate the strict Jensen equality condition.

Standard background, stated with sources

  • Leibniz determinant expansion: determinants are sums over permutations; each permutation decomposes uniquely into vertex-disjoint cycles.
  • Block-diagonal padding: adjoining absorbing states adds eigenvalues equal to one but preserves every eigenvalue of the original stochastic block.

Proved on this page

We derive the characteristic-polynomial expansion over disjoint directed cycles, classify every directed cycle of the sparse realization graph, and compute its characteristic polynomial in both signs of s−dq, realize every point on the Topic IX candidate curve at order at most n, and only then close the constant-parameter conclusion deferred from Topic X.

Notation recalled

The attainable region and its radial maximum

Here ΘN is the set of all eigenvalues of row-stochastic matrices of order N, and RN(θ) is its maximal radius on the ray of argument θ. We write ρ=RN(θ) for an actual radial maximum and ρ* for the candidate radius constructed in Topic IX.

Matrix convention

A weighted graph is a stochastic matrix with its rows drawn

We use the tail-row convention: a directed edge u→v of weight w is stored as follows.

Aᵤᵥ=w(u,v). (II.7.1)

Thus the entries in row u are the weights leaving vertex u. The matrix is row-stochastic exactly when all edge weights are nonnegative and the weights leaving each vertex sum to one.

In the Leibniz expansion of det(tI−A), a nonzero term selects pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles. Every unused vertex contributes a diagonal factor t. Lemma II.7.1 derives the sign and exponent directly from the permutation expansion.

XI.A

Directed-cycle expansion and graph classification

First the determinant rule is proved from Leibniz. Then the sparse realization graph is checked so that no unlisted directed cycle can contribute to its characteristic polynomial.

Lemma II.7.2 mentions the realization theorem that follows only to identify where its generic graph lemma will be applied. Its hypotheses describe the graph completely, and its proof does not assume the later theorem.

  1. II.7.1
    Lemma II.7.1Classical result

    Directed-cycle expansion of the characteristic polynomial

    Derives exactly how pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles contribute signs, edge weights, and powers of t to a characteristic polynomial.

    Definitions and notation used here4 entries to consult as needed
    Graph order N
    The total number of vertices of the finite directed graph. If selected cycles use L vertices altogether, the remaining N−L vertices supply diagonal factors t.
    Weighted adjacency matrix
    An edge u→v of weight w is stored in Aᵤᵥ, so row u lists the weights of edges leaving u.
    Directed simple cycle
    A closed directed route that repeats no vertex except its starting vertex at the end. A loop has length one.
    Vertex-disjoint cycle collection
    A set of cycles sharing no vertex. Vertices not selected on a cycle contribute diagonal factors t.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionA cycle of any length contributes one minus sign: its permutation sign and the minus signs from the selected matrix entries always multiply to −1.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma II.7.1 (Directed-cycle expansion of the characteristic polynomial). Let Γ\Gamma be a weighted directed graph on NN vertices, with adjacency matrix AA and edge-weight function ww. Then det⁡(tIN−A)=∑𝒞(−1)|𝒞|tN−|V(𝒞)|∏e∈E(𝒞)w(e),\det(tI_N-A) =\sum_{\mathcal C} (-1)^{|\mathcal C|} t^{N-|V(\mathcal C)|} \prod_{e\in E(\mathcal C)}w(e), where the sum ranges over all collections 𝒞\mathcal C of pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles in Γ\Gamma, including the empty collection. Here V(𝒞)V(\mathcal C) and E(𝒞)E(\mathcal C) are the unions of the vertices and edges of the selected cycles. Loops are allowed, and the empty product is one.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. Expand the determinant by Leibniz and then expand every selected diagonal factor t−Auut-A_{uu}. Each resulting monomial is obtained by selecting a collection of pairwise vertex-disjoint permutation cycles, with every remaining fixed vertex contributing tt. A directed cycle u1→u2→⋯→uℓ→u1u_1\to u_2\to\cdots\to u_\ell\to u_1 has permutation sign (−1)ℓ−1(-1)^{\ell-1}, while selecting the −A-A entry at its ℓ\ell vertices contributes (−1)ℓ(-1)^\ell. Its net sign is therefore −1-1, independently of ℓ\ell. For ℓ=1\ell=1, this says precisely that selecting the loop chooses −Auu-A_{uu} rather than tt from t−Auut-A_{uu}. Thus a collection of |𝒞||\mathcal C| cycles has sign (−1)|𝒞|(-1)^{|\mathcal C|}, contributes the product of its edge weights, and leaves N−|V(𝒞)|N-|V(\mathcal C)| diagonal factors tt. Summing over all collections, including the empty one, gives the formula. ◻

  2. II.7.2
    Lemma II.7.2

    Cycle collections in the sparse realization graph

    Proves that the designed graph has no cycles beyond the local q-cycles and one global s-cycle.

    Definitions and notation used here4 entries to consult as needed
    Block path
    A directed path in which every nonterminal vertex has one outgoing edge, of weight one.
    Within-block return edge
    The terminal edge of weight β that returns to the first vertex of the same block.
    Inter-block edge
    The terminal edge of weight α that enters the next block.
    Subdivision vertex
    A new vertex inserted on one inter-block edge to lengthen the cycle through all blocks without adding a choice.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionAt every block terminal a simple cycle must commit: take the within-block return edge and close inside that block, or take inter-block edges all the way around every block.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma II.7.2 (Directed cycles in the realization digraph). Let d,q∈ℕd,q\in\mathbb N. For each j∈ℤ/dℤj\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z, let Bj={vj,0,…,vj,q−1}B_j=\{v_{j,0},\ldots,v_{j,q-1}\} be a set of vertices, with the sets BjB_j pairwise disjoint, and put in the directed path vj,0→vj,1→⋯→vj,q−1.v_{j,0}\longrightarrow v_{j,1}\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow v_{j,q-1}. From each terminal vertex vj,q−1v_{j,q-1} add a return edge to vj,0v_{j,0} and an inter-block edge to a specified vertex vj+1,aj+1v_{j+1,a_{j+1}} of the next block, where 0≤aj+1<q0\leq a_{j+1}<q. At most one inter-block edge may instead be replaced by a directed path through new vertices, each of which has its unique outgoing edge to the next vertex of that path. When d=1d=1 and a0=0a_0=0, require this inter-block connection to be subdivided, so that it is distinct from the return edge.

    Then every directed simple cycle is either the within-block qq-cycle Cj:vj,0→⋯→vj,q−1→vj,0C_j:v_{j,0}\to\cdots\to v_{j,q-1}\to v_{j,0} for some jj, or the unique directed cycle C*C_* containing all dd inter-block connections. The cycle C*C_* meets every CjC_j at the terminal vertex of block jj. Consequently the collections of pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles are exactly

    1. an arbitrary subset of {C0,…,Cd−1}\{C_0,\ldots,C_{d-1}\}, or

    2. the singleton collection {C*}\{C_*\}.

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. A directed cycle using no inter-block edge is confined to one block. The directed path forces it to the terminal vertex, and the only edge that both remains in that block and closes a cycle is the return edge. Hence it is CjC_j.

    Now let a simple cycle use an inter-block connection into block jj. From its entry vertex vj,ajv_{j,a_j}, the directed suffix is forced until the terminal vertex. If the cycle then used the return edge, the path from vj,0v_{j,0} would reach the already visited entry vertex before the cycle could return through its preceding inter-block connection, contradicting simplicity. Thus it must use the inter-block connection at every terminal it reaches. These connections advance cyclically through all dd blocks, so they determine the unique cycle C*C_*. Any subdivision vertices lie on that cycle and create no additional choice.

    The cycle C*C_* contains the terminal vertex of every block, which also belongs to CjC_j. Thus C*C_* is not vertex-disjoint from any CjC_j, whereas the cycles CjC_j in distinct blocks are pairwise vertex-disjoint. This is the asserted classification. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      No inter-block edge means one block cycle
      The directed path reaches the terminal, whose only within-block exit is the return edge. The entire q-cycle is forced.
    2. 2
      An inter-block edge fixes the suffix
      After entering a block, the directed path forces the cycle to that block’s terminal.
    3. 3
      A later local return would repeat a vertex
      The local path would reach the already visited entry point before the original cycle closed, contradicting simplicity.
    4. 4
      All later exits are inter-block
      Repeating the same reasoning moves through all d blocks and creates the unique cycle containing every inter-block edge.
    5. 5
      Classify disjoint collections
      Distinct local cycles live in disjoint blocks. The global cycle visits every terminal, so it intersects every local cycle.

    ConclusionThe allowed collections are exactly an arbitrary subset of local cycles or the singleton global cycle.

Two graph regimes

Why the construction splits at s=dq

The target polynomial contains local cycles of length q and one global cycle of length s. With d local blocks, the unmodified cycle containing all inter-block edges has length dq. If s≤dq, inter-block edges enter some blocks part-way through and shorten that route. If s>dq, deterministic subdivision vertices lengthen one inter-block edge. Neither operation changes the local q-cycles.

The construction uses N₀=max(dq,s) states before padding. Farey arithmetic gives both dq≤n and s≤n, so the construction never uses more than n states. Absorbing-state padding then reaches exactly order n when necessary.

XI.B

Sparse stochastic realization and attainment

The theorem handles both regimes with an explicit graph, checks row sums and endpoint weights, and computes the exact reduced Ito polynomial. The following corollary inserts Topic IX's candidate.

Deferred conclusion

Only now do the two inequalities meet

Topic X proved ρ≤ρ* without assuming a stochastic matrix at the candidate radius. Corollary II.7.4 has now constructed that matrix, so maximality of ρ=RN(θ) gives the reverse inequality ρ*≤ρ. Equality is therefore forced, and the strict equality condition in Jensen forces all varying parameters to coincide.

XI.C

The outermost parameters are constant

Corollary II.6.2 appears here rather than in Topic X because its proof logically depends on the independent realization established immediately above.

  1. II.6.2
    Corollary II.6.2New result

    Constant parameters at an outer radial maximum

    Closes the deliberately deferred equality argument by comparing the actual extremum with the now-attained point from Topic IX.

    Definitions and notation used here2 entries to consult as needed

    Proof. Let μ\mu, ϑ\vartheta, yy, and the consecutive Farey fractions be supplied by Theorem II.5.1.
