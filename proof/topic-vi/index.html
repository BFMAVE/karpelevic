Topic VI · Manuscript pages 41–58

A projective deformation and the first-return step Δ = 1

For N≥4, why does a hypothetical first-return step Δ>1 produce an invariant polygon forbidden by criticality?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

Under the standing assumption N≥4, an affine projective chart turns the selected boundary chain into a convex graph. Successive perspectivities define a real projective map, and its behaviour near a fixed point supplies a small signed deformation into the polygon-interior half-plane.

The return-time bijection defines all N deformed vertices. Exact identities and finitely many open inequalities make the resulting polygon invariant, while one extreme point of its image lies in the polygon interior. Topic II rules this out for an N-critical map, so the first-return step is Δ=1.

Formal setup used below

Notation and exact facts imported from Topics II–V

Throughout Topic VI, N≥4. Let V be the underlying two-dimensional real vector space and let T:V→V be the fixed invertible real-linear contraction. After choosing the complex coordinate from Topic I, identify V with ℂ so that Tz=λz for a nonreal number λ with |λ|<1. For a compact convex set Q⊂V, Ext(Q) denotes its set of extreme points and int(Q) its interior in V. The map T is N-critical: N is the smallest number of vertices of a nondegenerate invariant polygon, and for every real t>1 the enlarged map tT has no invariant polygon with at most N vertices. Following Definition 1.2, every polygon named below has nonempty interior and is displayed by a cyclic list containing every extreme point exactly once and no other points. The polygon P=conv{x0,…,xN−1}P=\operatorname{conv}\{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\} has exactly these extreme points in counterclockwise order for the chosen complex orientation and satisfies λP⊆P. Its side with label i is Ei=[xi−1,xi]E_i=[x_{i-1},x_i]; all polygon indices are read modulo N. Write ℰ(P)={Ei:i∈ℤ/Nℤ} for the cyclic set of sides of P.

Topic IV supplies contact points ξi=λxi−κ∈Ei\xi_i=\lambda x_{i-\kappa}\in E_i, where 1≤κ<N throughout the deformation argument. (The final assembly of Theorem 1.3 treats the identity representative κ=N separately.) After cyclic relabelling, the side indices whose contacts lie in the relative interiors of their sides form B={1,…,φ}\mathcal B=\{1,\ldots,\varphi\}. Put δ=gcd(N,κ); δ is the number of orbits of addition by κ on the N cyclic labels. Topic VI treats the case φ>δ and proves that its first-return step is one. For a nondegenerate segment [a,b], relint[a,b] means the open segment obtained by deleting its two endpoints.

Topic V proves that the first-return map on 𝓑 is the translation r(j)=j+Δ(modφ)r(j)=j+\Delta\pmod\varphi. Its inverse is s. Topic V proves that there are integers q>0 and h≥0 such that every return time Hj belongs to {q,q+h}; when h=0 there is only one distinct height. Moreover, F(t,j)=[j+tκ]N,1≤j≤φ,0≤t<HjF(t,j)=[j+t\kappa]_N,\qquad 1\le j\le\varphi,\ 0\le t<H_j is a bijection onto the N polygon labels. Thus every polygon vertex has one and only one return-time representation.

To rule out Δ>1, Topic V selects distinct consecutive boundary vertices X0,…,Xm+1 that do not traverse the whole polygon, contact points Ci in relint[Xi−1,Xi], and supporting lines ℒi meeting P only at Xi. These are exactly the boundary-contact data of Definition 7.4, whose successive perspectivities define the line-to-line projectivity used below. Topic V also selects a set M of moved base indices, one distinguished base b*, and a disjoint exhaustive partition B=D⊔R⊔{c}⊔A,c=r(b*).\mathcal B=D\mathbin{\dot\cup}R\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{c\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}A,\qquad c=r(b_*). For k∈D the assigned side line moves and its source is fixed; for k∈R the source moves and the assigned side line is fixed; for k∈A both remain fixed. The incidence at c is the only one not imposed in advance. The forward and reverse formulas defining these sets are proved in Proposition 7.3.

Two earlier results supply the final logical safeguards. Lemma 2.8 from Topic II places any specified finite family of nonzero determinant signs, relative-interior incidences, and strict inequalities defining the required side half-planes on one common small parameter interval. Theorem 3.2 says that for every invariant polygon Q with at most N vertices, every extreme point of λQ lies on ∂Q. Topic VI will construct an invariant polygon violating that conclusion if Δ>1.

    Extension of the deformation to all polygon vertices

    Assuming the return-time bijection and four-case partition from Topic V, defines every deformed vertex and verifies every incidence with side index k≠c and all remaining side inequalities.

    Index flow used in the proof

    A base index j determines the orbit segment (F(0,j),…,F(Hj−1,j)) and its return index r(j). The moved bases form M, b* is the distinguished moved base, and c=r(b*). The partition 𝓑=D⊔R⊔{c}⊔A records exactly which endpoint of the final incidence varies:
