Topic XIII · Manuscript pages 102–106

The Karpelevič theorem in Ito’s formulation

How do the upper bound, attainment, and nesting assemble into the complete stochastic eigenvalue region?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

The small orders are proved directly. For n≥4, the induction separates according as the order-n radial maximizer lies outside or inside Θₙ₋₁; the results of Topics X–XII prove Rₙ(θ)=Kₙ(θ) in both cases.

Continuity of the full-circle radial function, conjugation, star-shapedness with respect to the origin, and the unit-circle classification then identify the Farey-indexed curves with the complete topological boundary. This is where the classical theorem is finally stated and proved.

Objects fixed for the final argument

Stochastic regions, Farey intervals, and radial functions

Topic XIII uses the following notation from Topics VIII–XII.

Stochastic eigenvalue region. A real matrix A=(aij) is row-stochastic when aij≥0 and every row sums to 1. The set Θn consists of all eigenvalues of all real n×n row-stochastic matrices.

Farey intervals. Fn+ is the increasing list of reduced fractions p/q in [0,1/2] with q≤n. Consecutive fractions determine the open angular intervals used below through x=θ/(2π).

Actual and candidate radii. For 0≤θ≤π, Rn(θ) is the largest ρ≥0 for which ρeiθ∈Θn. On an open Farey interval, Kn(θ) is Topic IX's unique positive solution of the scalar radius equation; at a Farey endpoint it is defined to be 1. Until the theorem is proved, Kn is only a candidate for Rn.

Curves. For consecutive f<g in Fn+, Γf,g(n) is the closure of the corresponding curve Kn(θ)eiθ. At order three, the interval [1/3,1/2] additionally contributes the real boundary segment [−1,−1/2].

I · The topological lemma

A continuous radial function determines the boundary

The ray sections determine the set once continuity guarantees that all strictly shorter points are interior.

  1. II.9.1
    Lemma II.9.1Classical result

    Compact star-shaped sets with continuous radial function

    Prove the topological step that turns a positive continuous radial function into an exact description of the set and its boundary.

    Definitions and notation used here3 entries to consult as needed
    Star-shaped with respect to 0
    A set S is star-shaped with respect to 0 when z∈S and 0≤t≤1 imply tz∈S. Every attained point brings the entire segment from 0 to that point with it.
    Open an example
    A closed disk centred at 0 is star-shaped with respect to 0. An annulus is not, because points near its outer circle do not bring the missing segment near 0.
    Radial function ρₛ(u)
    For a direction u on the unit circle, ρS(u) is the largest r≥0 for which ru∈S. Compactness makes this maximum attainable.
    Topological boundary ∂S
    The points for which every sufficiently small open disk meets both S and its complement. Because S is compact and therefore closed, this is also S minus its Euclidean interior in the whole plane ℂ≅ℝ²—not a relative interior inside a line or curve.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionStar-shapedness determines the intersection with each ray. Continuity in the direction u then shows that every point strictly below ρS(u) has a Euclidean neighborhood inside S, whereas a radial endpoint cannot be interior because every outward displacement leaves S.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma II.9.1 (Compact star-shaped sets with continuous radial function). Let S⊂ℂS\subset\mathbb C be compact and star-shaped with respect to the origin, and suppose that ρS(u):=max⁡{r≥0:ru∈S},u∈𝕊1:={v∈ℂ:|v|=1},\rho_S(u):=\max\{r\geq0:ru\in S\}, \qquad u\in\mathbb S^1:=\{v\in\mathbb C:|v|=1\}, is positive and continuous on the circle. Then S={tρS(u)u:0≤t≤1,u∈𝕊1},∂S={ρS(u)u:u∈𝕊1}.\begin{equation} \label{karp:eq:compact-radial-set} S=\{t\,\rho_S(u)u:0\leq t\leq1,\ u\in\mathbb S^1\}, \qquad \partial S=\{\rho_S(u)u:u\in\mathbb S^1\}. \end{equation}(II.9.1)

    Complete proofOpen the manuscript proof and its guided explanation

    Proof. Compactness makes each maximum attainable, and star-shapedness with respect to the origin then says that the intersection of SS with the ray in direction uu is exactly {ru:0≤r≤ρS(u)}\{ru:0\leq r\leq \rho_S(u)\}. Taking the union over u∈𝕊1u\in\mathbb S^1 gives the first formula. A point ρS(u)u\rho_S(u)u cannot be interior, since a sufficiently small outward radial displacement would contradict maximality.

    Conversely, let z=ruz=ru, where u∈𝕊1u\in\mathbb S^1 and 0<r<ρS(u)0<r<\rho_S(u). Choose η>0\eta>0 such that r+3η<ρS(u)r+3\eta<\rho_S(u). By continuity of ρS\rho_S on 𝕊1\mathbb S^1, there is an open neighborhood UU of uu in 𝕊1\mathbb S^1 such that ρS(v)>r+2η(v∈U).\rho_S(v)>r+2\eta\qquad(v\in U). The direction map w↦w/|w|w\mapsto w/|w| is continuous on ℂ\{0}\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}. Hence there is 0<δ<min⁡{r/2,η}0<\delta<\min\{r/2,\eta\} such that |w−z|<δ⇒w|w|∈U.|w-z|<\delta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{w}{|w|}\in U. For such ww one also has |w|<r+η|w|<r+\eta, and therefore |w|<r+η<ρS(w|w|).|w|<r+\eta<\rho_S\!\left(\frac{w}{|w|}\right). The ray description puts the whole ball B(z,δ)B(z,\delta) in SS, so every nonzero point strictly below the radial function is interior.

    Finally, positivity and compactness of the circle give c:=min⁡u∈𝕊1ρS(u)>0c:=\min_{u\in\mathbb S^1}\rho_S(u)>0, so the disk |z|<c|z|<c lies in SS and 00 is interior. Since SS is compact, it is closed. Every point of SS is either a radial endpoint ρS(u)u\rho_S(u)u or an interior point, and the radial endpoints are not interior. Thus the second formula follows. ◻

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Describe every ray section exactly
      For each u∈𝕊¹, compactness attains ρS(u), star-shapedness includes every smaller radius, and maximality excludes every larger one. Hence S∩{ru:r≥0}={ru:0≤r≤ρS(u)}.
    2. 2
      Identify every strictly shorter point as interior
      Let z=ru with 0<r<ρS(u). Continuity gives a neighborhood U of u on which the radial function remains larger than r by a fixed margin. Continuity of the direction map w↦w/|w| away from 0 then gives a disk about z whose points have direction in U and modulus below the corresponding radial value. That disk lies in S.
    3. 3
      Separate the boundary endpoints from the interior
      Positivity and compactness of 𝕊¹ give a uniform lower bound, so 0 is interior. Each point ρS(u)u is not interior because a slightly larger point on the same ray is outside S. Since compactness makes S closed and every other point of S is interior, these radial endpoints are exactly ∂S.

    ConclusionUnder the stated hypotheses, S={tρS(u)u:0≤t≤1, u∈𝕊¹} and ∂S={ρS(u)u:u∈𝕊¹}.

    Classical result. This is a standard polar-coordinate fact for compact star-shaped planar sets. The manuscript states and proves the precise version needed here.

II · The induction base

The exceptional small orders are computed directly

Order three is not a cosmetic special case: its last nonreal arc ends at −1/2 while the outer point of the negative-real ray is −1.

  1. II.9.2
    Proposition II.9.2Previously known

    Orders one, two, and three

    Prove the induction base directly, including the exceptional real segment that makes order three unlike every later order.

    Definitions and notation used here5 entries to consult as needed
    Radial functions Rₙ and Kₙ
    Rn(θ) is the attained maximum modulus in Θn on the ray of angle θ. The function Kn(θ) is the candidate selected by Topic IX's scalar equation and the endpoint convention.
    Trace identities
    For spectrum 1, λ=ξ+iη, and λ̄, one has tr(A)=1+2ξ and tr(A²)=1+2(ξ²−η²). Nonnegative entries and Cauchy–Schwarz imply 3η²≤(1−ξ)².
    Cyclic permutation matrix C₃
    The row-stochastic matrix that sends state 1 to 2, 2 to 3, and 3 back to 1. Its eigenvalues are 1, ω, and ω̄, where ω=e2πi/3.
    Explicit matrix Mα
    The displayed row-stochastic matrix has characteristic polynomial (λ−1)(λ²+λ+α). Its two nontrivial roots trace the vertical sides of the triangle and the segment [−1,−1/2].
    Exceptional interval
    On [1/3,1/2], the nonreal root branch runs from ω to −1/2. The outer point at the endpoint ray θ=π is −1, so [−1,−1/2] is a separate real boundary segment.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionOrders one and two follow from their general matrix forms. At order three, trace inequalities place every nonreal eigenvalue in the triangle with vertices 1, ω, and ω̄. Two displayed row-stochastic matrix families attain its boundary and the additional real interval. The scalar equation then identifies the two upper nonreal boundary pieces with K3.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Proposition II.9.2 (Orders one, two, and three). One has Θ1={1},Θ2=[−1,1],\Theta_1=\{1\}, \qquad \Theta_2=[-1,1], and, with ω=e2πi/3\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}, Θ3=conv⁡{1,ω,ω¯}∪[−1,−1/2].\begin{equation} \label{karp:eq:theta3} \Theta_3= \mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{1,\omega,\overline\omega\} \cup[-1,-1/2]. \end{equation}(II.9.2)

    Guided proof
    1. 1
      Compute orders one and two
      The sole 1×1 row-stochastic matrix is [1]. A 2×2 row-stochastic matrix has rows (a,1−a) and (b,1−b); besides eigenvalue 1, its other eigenvalue is a−b, which runs through [−1,1].
    2. 2
      Parameterize a nonreal order-three spectrum
      A real 3×3 matrix with λ=ξ+iη also has λ̄, and row-stochasticity supplies eigenvalue 1. Nonnegative diagonal entries give tr(A)=1+2ξ≥0, hence ξ≥−1/2. The unit-disk bound gives ξ≤1.
    3. 3
      Derive the triangle inequalities
      Cauchy–Schwarz and entrywise nonnegativity give (tr A)²≤3∑aii²≤3 tr(A²). Substituting the two trace identities yields 3η²≤(1−ξ)². Together with −1/2≤ξ≤1, this is exactly the closed triangle conv{1,ω,ω̄}.
    4. 4
      Attain the first two triangular sides
      The convex matrix family (1−α)I+αC3 has eigenvalue (1−α)+αω, which runs along the chord [1,ω]. Real matrices give the conjugate chord [1,ω̄]. Star-shapedness with respect to 0 supplies every point between these boundary chords and 0.
    5. 5
      Attain the remaining two sides and the real segment
      For the displayed matrix Mα, det(λI−Mα)=(λ−1)(λ²+λ+α). When 0≤α≤1/4, one real root runs from −1 to −1/2. When 1/4<α≤1, its two nonreal roots run from −1/2 to ω and ω̄. The same real matrix contains both conjugate roots.
    6. 6
      Identify the curve on [0,1/3]
      The scalar equation becomes the ray equation for the segment λ=β+αω, where α+β=1. Its argument increases strictly with α, so each ray with 0<θ<2π/3 meets the segment once. Topic IX's uniqueness therefore identifies its modulus with K3(θ).
    7. 7
      Identify the nonreal curve on [1/3,1/2]
      Here the scalar equation is equivalent to λ(λ²−β)=α. Along λ=−1/2+iη, the argument decreases strictly from π to 2π/3 as η rises, so every open ray is met exactly once. The interval [−1,−1/2] lies on the endpoint ray and is not part of this nonreal branch.
    8. 8
      Record the exact induction base
      The upper bounds and realizations coincide, and the endpoint convention gives radius 1 at 1, ω, and −1. Therefore R3(θ)=K3(θ) for every 0≤θ≤π, including the jump at θ=π.

    ConclusionThe theorem is completely proved for n≤3, and the exact equality R3=K3 is ready for the induction step.

III · The classical theorem

The radial equality and the complete boundary

The theorem is stated with its original manuscript number from Section II.3. Its complete proof appears here only after all later ingredients have been established, matching the logical rather than the printed order.

  1. II.3.1
    Theorem II.3.1Classical result

    Karpelevič theorem in Ito’s formulation

    Identify every boundary arc, the complete region star-shaped with respect to 0, and all unit-circle points for every matrix order.

    Definitions and notation used here4 entries to consult as needed
    Block-diagonal inclusion
    If A is row-stochastic of order n−1, then A⊕[1] is row-stochastic of order n and retains every eigenvalue of A. Thus Θn−1⊆Θn.
    Two membership cases
    For λn=Rn(θ)eiθ, either λn∉Θn−1 or λn∈Θn−1. The first case uses Topics X–XI; the second uses induction and Topic XII.
    Karpelevič boundary arcs
    After the equality Rn=Kn is proved, the curves Γf,g(n) on consecutive Farey intervals are the actual upper boundary arcs, not merely candidate curves.
    Induction on n
    Proposition II.9.2 proves orders 1–3. For n≥4, assume Rn−1=Kn−1 and prove Rn=Kn first on open Farey intervals and then at their endpoints.
    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionFix a direction in an open order-n Farey interval. Attainment gives Kn≤Rn. If the maximizing point is outside Θn−1, Topics X–XI identify its modulus with Kn. If it lies in Θn−1, block-diagonal inclusion, induction, Topic XII's monotonicity, and attainment force the same equality. Endpoint matrices settle the remaining rays, and Lemma II.9.1 converts the resulting continuous radial function into the boundary.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Theorem II.3.1 (Karpelevič theorem in Ito’s formulation). For n=1n=1, one has Θ1={1}\Theta_1=\{1\}, and for n=2n=2, one has Θ2=[−1,1]\Theta_2=[-1,1].
