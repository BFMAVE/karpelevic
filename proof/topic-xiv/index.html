The complete order-seven example and an interactive boundary plot

What does the theorem produce at one concrete order, and how can a reader draw further orders for themselves?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

For order seven, the general theorem yields a complete table of consecutive Farey pairs, reduced Ito polynomials, and parametrized boundary arcs. The direction x = 3/8 shows the scalar calculation numerically from beginning to end.

The chapter documents the numerical boundary-plot implementation and publishes its source and regression tests. Its final section lets the reader select n and obtain the corresponding plot, with exact Farey data kept visibly distinct from floating-point root-finding and plotting.

This interactive computation illustrates the theorem but is not part of its logical proof. Topic XIII remains the endpoint of the necessary argument; Topic XIV lets readers inspect the complete construction at order seven and then explore other orders themselves.

Reading convention. Essential definitions appear before the first statement that needs them. Complete manuscript proofs are closed by default. When a result has an added line-by-line explanation, it appears inside the same disclosure as the proof.

Standard background, stated with sources

  • Bisection: the elementary root-finding method that repeatedly halves an interval on which a continuous function changes sign. Its error is at most the initial interval length divided by 2 to the number of iterations.
  • Complex polar coordinates: the identity λ=ρe^{2πix}, where ρ is the modulus and x is the angle measured in turns.

1 · The complete order-seven Farey table

Nine Farey intervals cover 0≤x≤1/2

For n≥1, Θn is the set of eigenvalues of all n×n row-stochastic matrices: matrices with nonnegative entries whose rows each sum to one.

Let u/v<w/y be consecutive Farey fractions of order n. Relabel the endpoints as p/q and r/s so that q<s. This is denominator order and need not agree with left-to-right order. Put d=⌊n/q⌋, e=s−dq.

For an argument x strictly between the two endpoints, set A=2π|qx−p| and B=(2π/d)|sx−r|. The modulus ρ is the unique number in (0,1) satisfying ρs/dsin A+ρqsin B=sin(A+B). Define α=ρs/dsin A/sin(A+B) and β=ρqsin B/sin(A+B); then α+β=1 and λ=ρe2πix is the corresponding boundary point.

After clearing a possible negative power of z, every interval has the reduced Ito polynomial Pα(z)=zmax(e,0)(zq−β)d−αdzmax(−e,0). Within one open interval the integers p,q,r,s,d,e remain fixed; the angular gaps A,B, modulus ρ, coefficients α,β, and boundary point λ vary smoothly with x.

2 · One ray from beginning to end

The worked direction x=3/8

This calculation separates the exact choices from the numerical solve. Every fraction, determinant, and exponent below is exact; only the decimal radius, coefficients, and complex coordinates are approximations.

  1. 1

    Locate the Farey interval

    Since 1/3<3/8<2/5, the argument belongs to the interval with denominator-ordered data q=3 and s=5. The determinant check 3·2−1·5=1 confirms that the endpoints are Farey neighbours.

  2. 2

    Compute and clear the exponent

    The order is n=7, so d=⌊7/3⌋=2 and e=5−2·3=−1. For z≠0 the corresponding Laurent equation is z−1(z3−β)2=α2. Multiplication by z removes the negative power and gives (z3−β)2−α2z=0.

  3. 3

    Measure the angular gaps

    With p=1, q=3, r=2, s=5, and d=2, the definitions give A=2π|3·(3/8)−1|=π/4 and B=π|5·(3/8)−2|=π/8.

  4. 4

    Prove that the scalar equation has one root

    Let g(ρ)=ρ5/2sin(π/4)+ρ3sin(π/8)−sin(3π/8). Then g(0)<0<g(1), while g′(ρ)=(5/2)ρ3/2sin(π/4)+3ρ2sin(π/8)>0 for 0<ρ<1. The intermediate value theorem gives existence and strict monotonicity gives uniqueness. Bisection on [0,1] therefore converges to this root.

    Numerical check against the manuscript. ρ=0.940100221928822853… . The downloadable routine performs ninety bisection updates; in exact arithmetic the bracket would then have width 2−90. In the browser, IEEE-754 double precision limits trustworthy decimal accuracy to roughly fifteen digits, which is the regression tolerance.

  5. 5

    Recover α, β, and the boundary point

    Define α=ρ5/2sin(π/4)/sin(3π/8) and β=ρ3sin(π/8)/sin(3π/8). The scalar equation gives α+β=1. Numerically, α=0.655850787368397414… and β=0.344149212631602586… .

    Since 2πx=3π/4, λ=ρe3πi/4=−0.664751241920848908…+0.664751241920848908…i. Substitution gives (λ3−β)2=α2λ.

  6. 6

    Realize the point by a stochastic matrix

    Topic XI’s construction gives a 6×6 row-stochastic matrix. Its direct sum with the 1×1 matrix [1] is row-stochastic of order seven. A direct determinant calculation gives the characteristic polynomial (t−1)((t3−β)2−α2t), so substitution confirms that λ is an eigenvalue.

3 · Reproducible computation

Source and regression tests

The dependency-free module uses integer Farey arithmetic and a fixed ninety-step bisection. Its public helper validates that an input pair is consecutive in the Farey sequence of the supplied order, and the implementation includes the exceptional order-three real segment.

Exact combinatorial data

Reduced fractions, Farey-neighbour tests, denominator ordering, d, e, and rational endpoint arguments.

Floating-point data

Trigonometric values, interior radii, complex coordinates, finite sampling, and SVG coordinates.

Regression check

The published test reproduces the x=3/8 radius within 10−15 and checks the reduced polynomial residual.

Interactive numerical boundary plot

Choose n and plot an approximation to ∂Θn

Enter an integer from 1 to 40. Orders 1 and 2 use their exact elementary descriptions. From order 3 onward, each curved arc is sampled from the proved scalar equation using ninety bisection steps per sample. At order 3, the segment [−1,−1/2] on the real axis is inserted exactly.

Farey pairs and reduced Ito-polynomial data

Numerical output

Modulus and plotted curve

Interior moduli are found by fixed-iteration bisection. The SVG joins finitely many sampled points, so it is a numerical plot of the proved boundary formula, not an exact symbolic curve.
