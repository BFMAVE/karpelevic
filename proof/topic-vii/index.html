Topic VII · Manuscript pages 4–5 and 58–67

Consecutive Farey fractions and the finite product equation for N≥4

For N≥4, how do the first-return cases from Topic VI yield consecutive Farey fractions, a finite product equation, and an equality for chosen real arguments?

Topic orientationWhat this chapter proves and why it comes here

The first-return structure completed in Topic VI is converted into intervals between consecutive Farey fractions. When the opposite complex orientation is needed, every equation in the finite recurrence is conjugated and reversed, and its chosen real arguments are recomputed.

Theorem 1.4 and equations (1.5)–(1.9) are stated before the three return cases that prove them. Those cases produce the same finite product equation with possibly different parameters βⱼ, and consecutive vertex angles place each chosen factor argument in [A,M).

This completes the conditional geometric theorem for N-critical maps. Topic VIII returns to stochastic eigenvalue regions and proves that a radial boundary point new at order N satisfies that N-critical hypothesis.

Reading convention. The notation inherited from Topic VI is stated locally. The target theorem appears before the case analysis, while its proof assembly is deferred to the final disclosure. All eight result statements and all eight complete proofs remain present exactly once. Every equality of arguments is an equality between specified real lifts, not merely a congruence modulo 2π.

Formal setup used below

Notation and return cases imported from Topics I–VI

Throughout Topic VII, N≥4. Let V be the underlying two-dimensional real vector space and let T:V→V be the fixed N-critical elliptic contraction. In the adapted complex coordinate of Topic I, T acts as multiplication by one of two conjugate nonreal eigenvalues. We write λ for the eigenvalue in the contact orientation inherited from Topic VI, set θ=arg+(λ), and write x=θ/(2π). The final product may instead use μ=λ̄ after an explicit reflection. For a nonzero complex number z that is not positive real, arg+(z) denotes the unique argument in (0,2π).

Let P be the invariant N-gon and let κ∈{1,…,N} be the integer lift of its contact permutation. The value κ=N represents the identity permutation. In the nonidentity case, 1≤κ<N and δ=gcd(N,κ) is the number of contact-permutation orbits. After cyclic relabelling, the relative-interior contact indices form 𝓑={1,…,φ}. Topic VI leaves exactly three disjoint and exhaustive cases: κ=N; κ<N with φ=δ; or κ<N with φ>δ. In the last case the first-return map on 𝓑 is the cyclic shift j↦j+Δ modulo φ, and Topic VI proves Δ=1.

With Ei=[xi−1,xi], the assigned contact point has the coefficient form ξi=λxi−κ=βixi−1+ αixi, where αi>0, βi≥0, and αi+βi=1. Thus 0<βi<1 (equivalently αi,βi>0) is a relative-interior contact, while βi=0 is the retained endpoint xi. When the contact permutation is the identity, this reads λxi=βixi−1+ αixi; after a q-step return it becomes the factor relation (λq−βj)xj−1= αjxj. The lifted vertex arguments Θi are real representatives increasing along the chosen polygon boundary; their exact, rather than modular, differences supply the equalities for chosen real arguments below.

The Farey sequence FN is the increasing list of reduced fractions in [0,1] with denominator at most N. A Farey interval is the open interval between two consecutive terms of FN. Topic VII translates each of the three return cases above into one such interval, a finite product equation with parameters βj, and an equality for chosen real arguments of its factors.

    Farey adjacency criterion

    Recognizes neighbouring reduced fractions through a determinant of absolute value one.

    Lemma 8.2Classical result

    Reflection preserves Farey adjacency

    Reflects an interval between consecutive Farey fractions while preserving adjacency.

    Before reading the statementOpen the geometric idea and the role of the result

    IntuitionReflection changes orientation but not the lattice determinant or the denominator bound. It is the exact tool for choosing the conjugate eigenvalue while keeping an interval between consecutive Farey fractions.

    Complete manuscript statement

    Lemma 8.2 (Reflection preserves Farey adjacency). Let a/ba/b and c/dc/d be consecutive fractions of FNF_N, with a/b<c/da/b<c/d. Then (d−c)/d(d-c)/d and (b−a)/b(b-a)/b are consecutive fractions of FNF_N, and whenever a/b<x<c/da/b<x<c/d one has d−cd<1−x<b−ab.\begin{equation} \label{eq:reflected-farey-cell} \frac{d-c}{d}<1-x<\frac{b-a}{b}. \end{equation}(8.2) The reflected open interval is therefore again bounded by consecutive fractions of FNF_N. In particular, if d≤bd\leq b, the reflected ordered interval has the smaller denominator first.
